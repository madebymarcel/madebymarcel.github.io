How it works

Each team shares a policy with learned numerical weights. Every player observes their own boat, the match clock and what it can see or briefly remember. It chooses a paddle stroke and lets the stroke finish instead of steering directly every tick. Players select whom to pass to; the ball travels through the air and can be intercepted.

Learning

A Web Worker runs the same fixed-step simulator without rendering. A linear actor-critic updates its policy from experience: goals, fouls, winning a loose ball and completing passes that advance play or beat pressure. After one training match, a frozen candidate plays a separate evaluation against its parent. The result is saved regardless of who wins.

Rules and limitations

This prototype uses the 2025 ICF rules as a reference. It implements two ten-minute halves, five players per side, elevated goal detection, the individual possession limit, shot clock and basic restarts. Tied matches remain draws.

The referee does not yet judge kayak or hand tackles, paddle fouls, advantage, cards, exclusions or goalkeeper protection. Collisions, catches, shot-clock resets and restart placement are simplified; the visual referee signal is illustrative, not an official signal. No claim of complete rules compliance is made.
